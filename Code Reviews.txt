WEEK 2 - How to work with public/private variables
13 August 2026 with Arie

Today Arie and I talked about how I have my public and private variables set. Arie taught me how public variables have
public read AND WRITE access. Thus, by default, most variables should be set to private, and then read/write access is
provided through getters and setters. We also looked at how such variables are handled in Python vs. lower-level
languages like C++, where, for example, when using linked lists, the separate nodes can naturally be "separated" from
the rest of the memory, meaning it's OK for them to be public. I also had the opportunity to consider adding more input
validations within my constructors.

My next steps are to change my design to change most of the variables to private attributes. Then I will add getters
(and setters where appropriate) to control the public interface of the classes.

WEEK 3 - Reviewing SOLID principles and OOP design
20th August 2026 with Arie

In this session Arie and I discussed the current design of my financial system. He explained to me each of the SOLID
principles, and we discussed that when writing code, it can be more useful to focus on where the principles are being
violated instead of where they're demonstrated. Although my system is still quite simple at the present time, we
looked at where there could potentially be design errors, and we also discussed future plans such as implementing
proper error raising, and how we will expand the system in coming weeks. For example, I thought that when classes
assigned themselves IDs in addition to managing information about themselves, it may have violated the Single
Responsibility Principle (SRP), but Arie said if the classes are managing their own IDs then they were not particularly
violating anything -- although it could be an issue if, say, the Branch class managed the IDs for the Client class.

Arie also suggested that it's generally better to keep classes as separate as possible, or at least try to make them not
depend on each other too much. Another thing that we discussed was whether class attributes (outside of the instance)
should be included in the UML diagrams. Arie explained how it may be better to not include them as they are not really
relevant to the user, but he urged me to make a post on Ed Discussion about it for extra opinions.

I learned a lot about SOLID principles and how to apply them. In fact, Ari said it's somewhat difficult to violate
the principles if you're giving planning due process. As I move forward, I will take his advice to compare my code to
the SOLID principles from time to time and check whether any principles have been violated or not.
